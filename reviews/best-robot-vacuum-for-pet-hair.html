Suction, Brushes and Hair Wrap: What Actually Removes Pet Hair

Suction is measured in Pascals (Pa), and the numbers have climbed fast. In 2026, flagship robots advertise 15,000 Pa and more, while mid-range models sit around 8,000–12,000 Pa. For pet hair, our research suggests treating 8,000 Pa as a sensible minimum and 10,000 Pa or more as the comfortable zone, especially if you have medium- or high-pile carpet. Below that, fur tends to sit on top of the carpet rather than being lifted out, and you will see tumbleweeds of hair return within a day.

Brush design is the other half of the equation. Dual rubber brushes, like those on the Roomba Max 715 Vac, flex and counter-rotate so hair slides toward the ends instead of winding tightly around the middle. The Dreame L60 Ultra PE takes a different approach with its detangling DuoBrush, which is designed to actively resist tangles from long human and pet hair. The eufy C28 pairs its 15,000 Pa suction with a self-rinsing roller, a useful combination for homes where muddy paws follow the shedding. Whichever model you choose, plan on a quick brush check once a week in a heavy-shedding home — even the best detangling design is not maintenance-free.

Carpet boost is a feature worth checking in the app. Good robots detect carpet and automatically raise suction, then drop back down on hard floors to save battery and reduce noise. That matters for pet owners because the dirtiest surfaces in the house are usually the rugs in the living room and the carpet by the bed, exactly where pets sleep. If your home is mostly hard floors with a few rugs, a strong-suction model with good edge cleaning may matter more than raw carpet depth performance.

Self-Emptying Docks, Obstacle Avoidance and Living With a Robot

A self-emptying dock changes how a robot vacuum fits into a pet home. Without one, the small onboard bin fills with fur in a single run and the robot either stops or keeps running with reduced pickup. With one, the robot empties itself after each run into a sealed bag that typically lasts several weeks, even with pets. The Roomba Max 715 Vac bundle includes the AutoEmpty dock, which is why it leads our list on value: you get the feature that matters most for pet owners without paying flagship prices. The Roborock Qrevo Edge 3 Pro and Dreame L60 Ultra PE go further with wash-and-fill docks that also handle mopping, at a higher price.

Obstacle avoidance is the difference between a robot that cleans while you are out and one that needs rescuing. Pet homes are full of hazards: chew toys, socks, charging cables, and the occasional accident. Camera- and laser-based avoidance on the Dreame and Roborock flagships is designed to steer around small objects rather than plough through them. Cheaper models rely on bump sensors and are more likely to get stuck or drag something. If you have a puppy or a cat that knocks things over, obstacle avoidance is worth paying for. And no robot is a substitute for a quick tidy before a scheduled run — five minutes of picking up cords saves a lot of rescue missions.

Noise and scheduling deserve a thought too. The eufy C28 is the pick here for noise-sensitive homes: it is positioned as a quieter pet-home vacuum, which matters if the robot runs while you work from home or while a baby naps. Most of these robots let you schedule room-by-room cleaning, set no-go zones around food and water bowls, and run a quieter mode at night. Pair the vacuum with a good air purifier for large rooms and you tackle both the hair on the floor and the dander in the air — the two halves of pet-home cleanliness.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Suction: 8,000 Pa minimum for pet homes, 10,000 Pa or more for carpet.
  2. Brush type: Dual rubber or detangling brushes beat bristle brushes for hair wrap.
  3. Self-emptying dock: Strongly recommended for pet owners — check bag cost and capacity.
  4. Obstacle avoidance: Camera or laser avoidance if you have puppies, toys or clutter.
  5. Floor mix: Carpet boost for rugs, good edge cleaning for hard floors.
  6. Noise: Check decibel ratings if the robot will run while you work or sleep.
  7. Running costs: Bags, filters and brushes — budget for replacements every few months.
