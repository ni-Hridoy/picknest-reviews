Suction, Brushes and Scratch Safety on Wood Floors

Suction figures are quoted in Pascals, and on hardwood the numbers are less important than they look. A robot with 5,000 Pa and a good brush will pick up dust, crumbs and pet hair from a bare floor just as well as one with 10,000 Pa, because there is no carpet fiber resisting the airflow. What matters more is the brush: soft rubber rollers, like the dual counter-rotating rollers on the Roomba j9+, pick up hair without tangling and cannot scratch a sealed floor. Stiff bristle brushes can trap grit and drag it across the wood, so check the brush type before buying — and replace a worn brush promptly.

The Roborock Qrevo S uses a rubber main brush and 7,000 Pa of suction, a combination that handles dust, crumbs and pet hair on hardwood without drama. The eufy X10 Pro Omni goes further on paper with 8,000 Pa, while the Dreame L10s Ultra sits at 5,300 Pa but compensates with excellent mop pressure. On bare floors, all three will leave the floor visibly clean in a single pass; the differences show up on rugs and carpets, where higher suction earns its keep. If your home is mostly hardwood with a few area rugs, do not overpay for suction you will rarely use.

Scratch safety comes down to three things: soft brushes, rubber wheels, and clean maintenance. A robot that picks up a pebble or a piece of grit in its brush can drag it across the floor, so empty the dustbin regularly and check the brush for debris. Dark hardwood adds another wrinkle: some robots' cliff sensors mistake dark wood for a drop and refuse to clean it. LiDAR-based models like the Roborock and eufy navigate by laser rather than leaning on cliff sensors, and Roborock lets you adjust cliff-sensor sensitivity in the app — a real advantage on espresso-stained hardwood. If your floors are very dark, a LiDAR model is the safer choice.

Mopping Systems, Docks and Navigation

Mopping systems fall into three tiers. At the bottom, a damp pad dragged behind the robot — adequate for light dust, useless on dried spills. In the middle, vibrating pads, like the Shark's sonic mopping, which scrub at high frequency. At the top, spinning pads, like those on the Roborock Qrevo S and Dreame L10s Ultra, which rotate and press down on the floor for real scrubbing. Spinning pads are the clear winner on hardwood, and both top models lift their pads when they detect carpet, so your rugs stay dry. The Dreame L10s Ultra adds a mop-extending arm that reaches into corners and along baseboards, a detail that matters on wood floors where dust collects at the edges.

The dock is where the premium models justify their price. The Qrevo S, X10 Pro Omni and L10s Ultra all ship with docks that empty the dustbin into a bag, wash the mop pads, dry them with warm air and refill the water tank. That turns a daily chore into a weekly one: you empty a dust bag every month or two and refill the clean-water tank every few days. The Roomba j9+ dock is simpler — it empties dust into a bag but does not mop — and the Shark's base self-empties dust and holds water for mopping. Budget for consumables: dust bags, mop pads, filters and brushes all need replacing, typically $50–$100 a year.

Navigation decides whether the robot actually cleans the whole floor. LiDAR models build a precise map on the first run, clean in straight lines, remember multiple floors and work in the dark — which is why the Roborock, eufy, Dreame and Shark all use it. The Roomba j9+ uses camera-based vSLAM navigation instead, which adds genuinely useful obstacle avoidance: its front camera recognizes cords, socks and pet waste and steers around them. That is a real advantage in a cluttered home or one with pets, but camera navigation can struggle in low light. For most hardwood homes, LiDAR is the better default; for pet owners, the Roomba's camera earns its keep.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Brush type: Soft rubber rollers are safest on sealed hardwood; avoid stiff bristles.
  2. Mopping: Spinning mop pads that lift on carpet beat a dragged damp pad.
  3. Navigation: LiDAR maps accurately and works in the dark; camera adds obstacle avoidance.
  4. Dark floors: Check cliff-sensor behavior — LiDAR models handle dark wood best.
  5. Dock: Auto-empty and mop-washing docks cut maintenance to minutes a week.
  6. Consumables: Budget $50–$100 a year for bags, pads, filters and brushes.
  7. Floor mix: Mostly hardwood favors a mop combo; mostly carpet favors a vacuum-first model.
