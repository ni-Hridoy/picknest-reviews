The Bowflex Recall: What You Need to Know Before Buying

This is the most important section in this guide, so let us be direct. In June 2025, the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recalled the original Bowflex SelectTech 552 and 1090 dumbbells — about 3.8 million units — because weight plates could dislodge from the handle during use. The recall followed hundreds of reports and more than a hundred reported injuries. Bowflex has since redesigned the line as the "Results Series," which is the version currently sold. If you buy new from a reputable retailer, you should receive the Results Series, but check the product name on the listing before you order.

The recall matters most if you buy used. A second-hand pair of original 552s or 1090s may still be in circulation, and those are the units covered by the recall. Before buying any used Bowflex dumbbells, find the model and serial number on the sticker under the tray and check it against the CPSC recall notice. If the pair is covered, do not use it — the manufacturer offered remedies for affected owners. The redesign does not change the other honest limitation of dial dumbbells either: they are not designed to be dropped, and the length of the bell at light weights can feel awkward on moves like chest presses.

None of the other brands in this guide — PowerBlock, NÜOBELL — appeared in a CPSC dumbbell recall search, which is worth knowing but not a guarantee. Every adjustable dumbbell is a mechanism, and every mechanism has a failure mode. The practical rule is simple: set weights down gently, check that plates are seated before each set, and never drop an adjustable dumbbell the way you might drop a rubber fixed dumbbell.

Weight Range, Increments and Change Speed

Weight range decides whether a pair lasts you a year or five. Most home lifters are well served by 5 to 50 pounds per hand, which covers curls, presses, rows and lunges for the vast majority of people. The Bowflex 552 covers 5–52.5 lb in 2.5 lb steps up to 25 lb, then 5 lb steps — small increments matter because adding 5 pounds to a curl is a big jump, while 2.5 pounds is a realistic progression. The PowerBlock Elite EXP starts at the same 5–50 lb range but can be expanded with add-on kits to 70 and then 90 lb, which makes it the best choice if you expect to outgrow a 50-pound pair.

If you already lift heavy, start higher. The NÜOBELL 580 runs 5–80 lb in 5 lb steps with a twist of the handle — the fastest change in this guide and the closest feel to a fixed dumbbell, thanks to a knurled steel handle and an all-metal build. The Bowflex 1090 runs 10–90 lb with 17 settings, which suits deadlifts, heavy rows and goblet squats. Note the 1090's floor: it starts at 10 lb, not 5, so light warm-up work and rehab exercises need a lighter pair alongside it.

Change speed is the difference between a smooth workout and a frustrating one. Dial designs like the Bowflex let you twist and lift in a couple of seconds. Twist-handle designs like the NÜOBELL are similarly fast. Pin designs like the PowerBlock take a little longer — you pull a pin, move it and re-seat it — but the mechanism is simpler and more tolerant of rough handling, which is why many lifters prefer it. If you do drop sets or supersets with frequent weight changes, speed matters. If you do slow strength work with long rests, it barely does.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Recall check: Buy the Bowflex Results Series new; if buying used, verify the serial number against the June 2025 CPSC recall.
  2. Weight range: 5–50 lb covers most lifters; 80–90 lb if you deadlift or row heavy.
  3. Increments: 2.5 lb steps are far better for upper-body progression than 5 lb steps alone.
  4. Mechanism: Dial is fastest, twist handle feels most like a fixed dumbbell, pin is the most rugged.
  5. Single vs pair: Some listings sell one dumbbell — check whether the price is for a single or a pair before you order.
  6. Length: Adjustables are longer than fixed dumbbells; check the length at your usual working weight.
  7. Warranty: PowerBlock offers 5 years residential; Bowflex offers 2–3 years on parts; NÜOBELL offers 2 years, void if dropped.
