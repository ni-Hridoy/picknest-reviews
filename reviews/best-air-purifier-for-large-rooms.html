Every coverage figure in this guide uses the stricter standard unless stated otherwise.

That math is the key to buying right. The rule is simple: multiply the smoke CADR by 1.55 to get the maximum room size in square feet at 4.8 air changes per hour. A purifier with 391 smoke CADR covers about 606 sq ft; one with 233 CADR covers about 361 sq ft. Measure your room, do the math, and buy a purifier rated for at least your room size — ideally a bit more, because open-plan spaces, high ceilings and open doorways all add volume the purifier has to clean. Buying Guide: 5 Things to Check Before You Buy

  1. Do the CADR math: Smoke CADR × 1.55 = max room size in sq ft at 4.8 air changes per hour. Buy a purifier rated for at least your room size, ideally 20% more.
  2. True HEPA + carbon: Insist on True HEPA (not "HEPA-like") for particles, plus activated carbon for smoke, cooking and pet odors.
  3. Noise at usable speeds: Check the dB rating at medium and high — a purifier you turn off because it is loud cleans nothing. Under 30 dB on low is ideal for bedrooms.
  4. Filter costs and availability: Budget $50–$100 per year for replacements and confirm filters are easy to buy; proprietary filters cost more.
  5. Placement and features: Put the purifier where air circulates, away from walls and curtains; smart sensors, auto mode and a timer are worth paying for, and Black Friday is the best time to buy.

Frequently Asked Questions

CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate) measures how much clean air a purifier delivers, in cubic feet per minute. For large rooms you need a high CADR — generally 300 or more for smoke — because the purifier must cycle a large volume of air several times per hour to actually clean it.

Multiply the smoke CADR by 1.55 to get the maximum room size in square feet at 4.8 air changes per hour, the standard for allergy and asthma relief. For example, a 391 CADR purifier covers about 606 square feet. For a room larger than that, you need a bigger purifier or two units.

Most HEPA filters last 6–12 months and carbon filters 3–6 months, depending on use and air quality. Running a purifier on high during wildfire season shortens filter life. Check the filter indicator and buy replacements from the manufacturer to keep CADR performance intact.
