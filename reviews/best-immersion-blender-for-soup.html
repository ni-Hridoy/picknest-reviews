What Is an Immersion Blender, and How Does It Work?

An immersion blender — also called a stick blender or hand blender — is a long wand with a motor in the handle and a blade inside a bell-shaped guard at the bottom. You lower it directly into the pot, bowl or jug and blend where the food already is. The bell guard does two jobs: it keeps the blade from touching the pot, and it shapes the flow of liquid so food is drawn down into the blades instead of spraying up the sides. The best guards, like the Breville's ribbed bell and the Vitamix's bell, also break the suction that can glue a cheap stick blender to the bottom of a pot mid-blend.

For soup, the advantage is practical, not theoretical. A countertop blender forces you to ladle hot soup into a jug in batches, vent the lid so steam does not blow it off, and then wash the jug, the lid and the base. An immersion blender purees the whole pot in place in about a minute, and you control the texture by feel — pulse for chunky, hold for silky — then rinse the shaft and put it away. The technique matters: keep the head fully submerged, start on the lowest speed, tilt the blender slightly to pull food through the blades, and never lift it while the motor is running. That is the whole skill, and it takes one pot of soup to learn. The same tool also makes mayonnaise, whipped cream with the whisk attachment, and smoothies in a jar — but soup is where it earns its drawer space.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Match the power to your soup: 180 watts handles cooked vegetables; 625 watts handles thick bean and root-vegetable soups without slowing.
  2. Count the speeds: more speeds mean finer texture control — 15 on the Breville, a variable trigger on the KitchenAid, 5 on the Vitamix.
  3. Check the shaft length: an 8-inch shaft reaches the bottom of a tall stockpot; shorter shafts force you to tilt the pot.
  4. Look for a pan guard: a non-scratch guard protects enamel and nonstick pots from the blade housing.
  5. Decide on attachments: a whisk and chopper add real value if you will use them; if not, the Vitamix's bare box is the better buy.
  6. Buy in Black Friday week: small appliances see some of their deepest discounts in late November.
