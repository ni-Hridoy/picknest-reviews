Bowflex 840 vs PowerBlock vs Yes4All

The Bowflex SelectTech 840 is the fastest to adjust and the easiest to live with. Six settings — 8, 12, 20, 25, 35 and 40 lb — selected with a dial on top, a 2-year warranty, and a price around $150. The trade-offs are real: the shell is largely plastic, so it is not a bell to drop, and the weight jumps are uneven (8 to 12 is a 4 lb jump; 25 to 35 is 10 lb). For circuit training and general fitness, though, the dial is the difference between changing weights mid-workout and skipping the change.

The PowerBlock is the connoisseur's pick: an all-steel shell, a magnetic pin that locks the selected plates in place, four settings (18, 22, 26 and 35 lb) and a 5-year warranty. It feels like a real kettlebell in the hand, and its compact, flat-sided shape is comfortable for presses and cleans. The Yes4All is the budget champion: a cast iron handle and six 5 lb plates that stack to 10–40 lb, a wide textured grip and a flat base, for around $100. What Is an Adjustable Kettlebell, and How Does It Work?

A kettlebell is a cannonball-shaped weight with a handle, used for swings, goblet squats, presses, snatches and carries — the classic tool for full-body strength and conditioning. A fixed kettlebell weighs one thing; an adjustable kettlebell contains a stack of weight plates inside a shell, and a mechanism selects how many plates you lift. The Bowflex uses a dial that engages different plates, the PowerBlock uses a magnetic steel pin that locks plates into the shell, and the Yes4All uses plates you add or remove by hand.

The training case is simple: kettlebell exercises progress quickly, and a beginner who starts swinging 12 lb will want 20 lb within months. Buying fixed bells at every step costs more than an adjustable, and takes up far more space. The safety case matters just as much: because the plates are held by a mechanism, you must check that the weight is locked before every set — especially with a dial or pin system — and never drop an adjustable bell. Kettlebell training is also a genuine cardio workout: swings raise the heart rate fast, which is why adjustable bells pair well with cardio machines. Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Match the range to your strength: beginners need 8–12 lb options; the PowerBlock starts at 18 lb.
  2. Check the locking mechanism: dial, pin or plates — always verify it is locked before you lift.
  3. Never drop an adjustable bell: the mechanism is the weak point; set it down, do not drop it.
  4. Count the jumps: uneven jumps (8, 12, 20, 25, 35, 40) are fine for most training, but not for fine progression.
  5. Measure your grip: a wide handle suits two-handed swings; a narrow handle suits presses.
  6. Buy in Black Friday week: fitness gear sees strong discounts in late November.
