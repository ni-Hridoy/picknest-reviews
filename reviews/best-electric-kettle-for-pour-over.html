What Is Pour Over Coffee, and Why Does the Kettle Matter?

Pour over coffee is made by pouring hot water in slow circles over ground coffee in a paper filter, letting gravity pull the water through the grounds into a carafe below. The method gives you complete control over the two variables that decide flavour: water temperature and pour speed. Water that is too hot over-extracts and tastes bitter; water that is too cool under-extracts and tastes sour. A fast, uneven pour channels through the grounds and brews inconsistently.

That is why the kettle matters more in pour over than in any other brewing method. A gooseneck spout — long, thin and curved — restricts the flow to a thin, steady stream you can aim precisely, so you can wet the grounds evenly, bloom them for 30–45 seconds, then pour in slow spirals. Temperature control matters just as much: most pour over recipes call for 195–205°F, well below boiling, and a kettle that holds that temperature while you pour keeps every cup consistent. Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Pick your capacity: 0.8–0.9L suits one or two cups; 1.7L suits a household or a teapot.
  2. Decide presets vs precision: five presets cover almost everyone; degree control is for recipe chasers.
  3. Check the spout: only a true gooseneck gives the slow, aimable pour that pour over needs.
  4. Look for a hold function: 60 minutes of hold means the water is still at temperature for a second cup.
  5. Descale regularly: mineral build-up is the most common cause of kettle failure — the COSORI manual asks for weekly descaling with heavy use.
  6. Register the warranty: Fellow and COSORI both add a year of coverage when you register.
