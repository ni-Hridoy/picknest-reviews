What Is a Canister Vacuum? Canister vs Upright for Pet Hair

A canister vacuum splits the machine in two. The motor, bag and filter live in a wheeled canister body that trails behind you on the floor, while you hold only a lightweight hose, wand and floor head. An upright, by contrast, puts the motor, bin and brush head in one tall body that you push in front of you. That single design difference explains almost everything about how the two types feel in a pet home. With a canister, the part in your hand weighs very little, so cleaning stairs is a matter of carrying the wand up step by step while the canister sits below, and reaching under a low sofa or bed means sliding a slim floor head underneath rather than wrestling a tall body into the gap. Upholstery, car seats, curtains and ceiling corners are the same story: the hose reaches them, and the canister stays on the floor.

Uprights have their own strengths. For a large, open, carpeted room, an upright is often faster: you push one unit in straight passes, the brush head is always under power, and there is no canister to drag around corners. Many uprights are also cheaper and simpler to store. But uprights struggle exactly where pet hair hides: on stairs, where the whole body must be balanced on each step; under furniture, where the tall body cannot go; and on the sofa, where a heavy upright is awkward to lift. If you want a machine that also cleans the house rather than just the floors, our best robot vacuum for pet hair guide covers the hands-off option, and our best cordless vacuum for pet hair guide covers quick daily clean-ups — but neither replaces a canister for deep pet-hair cleaning on stairs and upholstery. For pet owners, the practical rule is simple: mostly open carpet and a tight budget points to an upright; stairs, mixed floors, furniture and a shedding pet point to a canister with a powered head.

Canisters also differ among themselves in ways that matter for pet hair. Bagged canisters seal hair and dander in a disposable bag, which is cleaner to empty and usually better for allergies, but bags cost money and need replacing. Bagless canisters save on bags but emptying a bin full of pet hair can puff dander back into the air. Powered brush heads, driven by their own motor through an electrified hose, beat air-driven turbo brushes on carpet, because they keep spinning at full speed even when suction is high. And filtration terminology varies: a true HEPA exhaust filter is a specific standard, while "HEPA-type", "S-Class" and "Active AirClean" are manufacturer terms that are not identical — check what each model actually ships with, which we do for each pick below.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Powered brush head first: For carpet, insist on an electric power head (electrobrush / ET-1 / Pet PowerMate), not just straight suction. Air-driven turbo brushes are a step down on medium- and high-pile carpet.
  2. Bagged vs bagless: Bagged canisters seal hair and dander for cleaner emptying and are usually better for allergies; budget for replacement bags and check the bag type before you buy.
  3. Filtration, read carefully: "HEPA", "S-Class" and "Active AirClean" are not the same thing. Confirm the exact filter in the box, and whether a HEPA option costs extra.
  4. Reach and weight: Check the operating radius (cord plus hose) against your largest room, and the body weight if you will carry it up stairs — the SEBO body is about 12 lb, the Miele and Kenmore around 22–23 lb as listed.
  5. Pet tools included: A hand turbobrush or motorised pet tool for stairs and upholstery is essential in a pet home; buying one separately is expensive.
  6. Noise: Pets startle. The SEBO is listed at 63 dBA, the Kenmore at 75 dB — a real difference in a small home.
