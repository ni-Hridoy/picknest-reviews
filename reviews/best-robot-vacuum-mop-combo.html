Mopping Systems: Rotating Pads, Vibration and Real Scrubbing Power

There are three mopping approaches in 2026, and they perform very differently. Basic models drag a damp pad. Mid-range models vibrate the pad or spin two rotating pads to scrub the floor. Flagships add downward pressure, hot-water washing and automatic pad lifting. For everyday dust, footprints and kitchen splashes, a rotating-pad system is a clear step up from a drag pad — it actually agitates the floor instead of just wiping it.

The Roborock Saros 20 is the flagship reference here, with 36,000 Pa of suction for the vacuum side and a mopping system designed to handle thresholds and rugs without intervention. Its AdaptiLift chassis climbs thresholds up to 3.46 inches, which matters in homes where rooms are separated by raised strips that stop lesser robots. The Dreame X60 Max Ultra Complete counters with 35,000 Pa and the thinnest flagship body at 3.13 inches, so it slides under low sofas and beds where dust and pet hair collect. If your home has low furniture, that height difference is more useful than a small suction advantage.

The mid-range and value picks are not far behind on everyday mopping. The Ecovacs Deebot T90 Pro Omni offers strong mopping with an auto-empty and auto-wash dock at a mid-range price, making it a good choice for homes that are mostly hard floors. The Roomba Plus 415 Combo is the budget-friendly route into the category: it vacuums and mops, and its AutoWash Dock washes the pads for you, which was a premium-only feature not long ago. Expect all of these to handle daily grime well and dried-on stains poorly — that is the honest limit of robot mopping in 2026.

Docks, Carpet Handling and Day-to-Day Living

The dock is where the real convenience lives. A basic dock charges the robot. A good combo dock empties the dustbin, washes the mop pads with hot water, dries them so they do not smell, and refills the clean-water tank. The Dreame X60 Max Ultra Complete and the Roborock Saros 20 sit at the top of this ladder, while the Ecovacs and iRobot docks cover the essentials at lower prices. Whatever you buy, budget a few minutes a week for the dock itself: empty the dirty-water tank, rinse the tray, and check the filter. A self-washing dock reduces hand-washing, it does not eliminate maintenance.

Carpet handling is the feature most buyers forget to check, and the one that causes the most regret. A combo that drags a wet pad across a rug will leave damp streaks and, over time, odours. Good models solve this in two ways: they lift the pads several millimetres when they detect carpet, or they detach the pads entirely and leave them at the dock before vacuuming carpeted rooms. The Roborock Qrevo line popularised automatic mop detachment, and the Saros 20 carries that thinking further. If your home is mostly carpet, a dedicated vacuum may still be the better buy — a combo earns its keep in homes with large hard-floor areas.

Navigation and scheduling round out the experience. All four picks map your home, let you set room-by-room routines, and support no-go zones around pet bowls and cables. A useful routine for hard-floor homes is to vacuum first, then mop the kitchen and entryway on a higher water setting, and skip the bedrooms on mop days. Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Mop type: Rotating or vibrating pads scrub; drag pads only wipe.
  2. Carpet logic: Pads must lift or detach on carpet — verify how high and how reliably.
  3. Dock: Self-washing and hot-air drying are worth paying for if you mop daily.
  4. Suction: 8,000 Pa or more keeps the vacuum side useful, not just the mop.
  5. Height and thresholds: Measure under-furniture clearance and door thresholds before buying.
  6. Water tanks: Larger clean and dirty tanks mean fewer refills on big floors.
  7. Running costs: Mop pads, filters, bags and cleaning solution add up — check prices first.
