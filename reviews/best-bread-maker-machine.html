What Is a Bread Maker, and How Does It Work?

A bread maker is a countertop appliance that mixes, kneads, proves and bakes a loaf of bread unattended. You put flour, water, yeast, salt and any extras into a removable nonstick pan, choose a program, and the machine does the rest: a paddle kneads the dough, a heater holds it at proving temperature, the dough rises, and then the machine bakes it — all inside the same pan. A basic white loaf takes about three to four hours; a rapid cycle can finish in about two and a half. The delay timer lets you load the ingredients at night and set the finish time for the morning, so the machine starts itself while you sleep.

The programs are where the machines differ. A basic cycle handles white and whole wheat; dedicated cycles adjust kneading time, rise time and temperature for gluten-free, sweet and French breads; a dough-only cycle kneads and proves without baking, so you can shape pizza dough, rolls or cinnamon buns by hand and bake them in your oven. That dough cycle is quietly the most useful feature of any bread maker — it turns the machine into a dough mixer for everything from pizza night to holiday rolls. The honest limit is that a bread maker bakes one shape of loaf, and a serious baker will eventually want an oven and a Dutch oven for artisan bread. But for everyday sandwich bread, a bread maker is the cheapest fresh bread you will ever eat.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Choose the pan shape first: horizontal for a proper sandwich loaf, vertical for a smaller, cheaper machine.
  2. Measure your counter: the Zojirushi is 18 inches wide and 24 lb; the Cuisinart tucks under a cabinet.
  3. Check the loaf sizes: 2 lb suits a family; 1 lb suits one or two people; the Breville stretches to 2.5 lb.
  4. Look for a dough cycle: it turns the machine into a pizza and roll dough mixer, doubling its value.
  5. Count the custom programs: only the Breville lets you program a true sourdough rise.
  6. Buy in Black Friday week: bread makers see strong discounts in late November, and make a great Christmas gift.
