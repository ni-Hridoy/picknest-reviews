Stride Length, Flywheel and Incline: The Specs That Decide Everything

Stride length is the single most important spec on an elliptical. It is the distance the pedals travel, and it decides whether the motion feels like natural walking or like shuffling in place. Most adults need 18 to 20 inches; 20 inches suits people from about 5 feet 2 inches to 6 feet 2 inches and is the standard on the Sole E35, the NordicTrack 14.9 and the Schwinn 430. The Horizon EX-59 offers 18 inches, which is fine for shorter users and smaller rooms but can feel cramped for taller people. If two people of very different heights will share the machine, a 20-inch stride — or an adjustable stride like the ProForm Carbon E10's — is the safer choice.

Flywheel weight decides smoothness. A heavier flywheel carries momentum through the dead spots in the pedal stroke, which is why the Sole E35's 25 lb flywheel feels so much smoother than the Horizon EX-59's 14.3 lb one. Lighter flywheels feel choppy at low resistance and can jerk at the top of the stroke. Resistance levels matter too: the Sole E35 offers 20 levels of electronic resistance, the Schwinn 430 offers 22 levels plus 10 incline positions, and the NordicTrack 14.9 offers 26 levels with automatic adjustment during iFIT classes. More levels mean finer control, which matters as your fitness improves.

Incline changes which muscles do the work. The Sole E35's power incline adjusts from 0 to 30 degrees, shifting the emphasis from quads to glutes as you raise it. The NordicTrack 14.9 offers a similar range with automatic adjustment during classes. The Horizon EX-59 has no power incline, which is one of the trade-offs for its lower price. If you want the most training variety from one machine, power incline is worth paying for; if you mostly want steady-state cardio, you will not miss it.

Touchscreens, Subscriptions and Living With an Elliptical

The console is where the brands split. Sole keeps it simple: the E35 has a 10.1-inch colour touchscreen, Bluetooth speakers and built-in programs, but no subscription is required to use the machine properly. NordicTrack and ProForm go the other way: the Commercial 14.9's 14-inch HD touchscreen and the Carbon E10's 10-inch screen are built around iFIT, with trainer-led classes, scenic routes and automatic resistance changes. The classes are genuinely motivating, but after the free trial they need a paid membership, and that ongoing cost should be part of your decision. If you already pay for a fitness app, or you just want to watch your own shows while you train, a Sole or Horizon is the better value.

Space and assembly are the practical realities. A full-size elliptical is roughly 70 inches long and 25–30 inches wide, and it needs clearance around it — check the manufacturer's footprint and add room to step on and off safely. These machines weigh 200 pounds or more, so assembly is a two-person job and moving one up stairs is a project. The Schwinn 430, when available, is a good example of a machine that punches above its price: a 20-inch stride, 22 resistance levels and 10 incline positions at a mid-range price, with a simple dual-track LCD rather than a touchscreen. Its patchy availability in 2026 is the main reason it is a secondary pick rather than a headline one — check Amazon and other retailers for current stock.

Warranties tell you what the manufacturer actually believes about its machine. Sole offers a lifetime frame warranty with 5 years on parts, which is among the best in the industry and a real reason to choose the E35. Horizon offers a lifetime frame warranty too, which is remarkable at its price. NordicTrack and ProForm warranties are shorter and vary by model, so read the fine print before buying. And remember the calendar: ellipticals are among the most discounted fitness machines during Black Friday Week, often by hundreds of dollars, and buying now beats paying full price in January when resolution demand peaks.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Stride: 20 inches for most adults; 18 inches minimum for shorter users; adjustable stride if two very different heights share the machine.
  2. Flywheel: 20 lb or more for a smooth feel; under 15 lb often feels choppy.
  3. Max user weight: A higher rating (300–350 lb) usually signals a stronger frame.
  4. Incline: Power incline adds training variety; manual or none is fine for steady cardio.
  5. Console: Decide before buying whether you want a subscription (iFIT) or a simple console with no ongoing cost.
  6. Footprint: Measure the room — about 70 x 30 inches plus clearance to mount and dismount.
  7. Warranty: Lifetime frame coverage is the gold standard; check parts and labour terms too.
