What Is an Air Fryer — and How Is It Different From a Convection Oven?

An air fryer is a compact convection cooker. A heating element and a powerful fan sit above a small, perforated basket, and the fan drives very hot air around the food at high speed. Because the chamber is tiny and the food sits close to the heat, the surface of the food dries and browns quickly — that is the crispiness air fryers are famous for, achieved with a spoonful of oil instead of a pot of it. A basket air fryer like the COSORI TurboBlaze 6.0-Qt does this in a 6-quart drawer you can shake halfway through cooking; the Ninja Foodi DZ201 doubles it with two independent 4-quart zones, each with its own fan and heater.

A convection oven uses the same principle — a fan circulating hot air — but spread across a full-size cavity with racks, a glass door and multiple heating elements. The Breville Smart Oven Air Fryer Pro adds a dedicated air fry function to a 13-function countertop oven, with its Element IQ system adjusting the heating elements for each mode; the Instant Omni Plus 18L offers 10 functions including rotisserie, with EvenCrisp technology driving airflow from the top down. Because the cavity is larger, the air moves more gently and the heat is spread over a bigger area — better for even baking and roasting, slower to crisp a small batch of fries.

The practical difference is heat density. In an air fryer, a small amount of food gets a lot of fast-moving hot air; in a convection oven, a large amount of food gets a moderate amount. That is why the same chicken wings come out crisper in the basket but a whole roast chicken only fits in the oven. Neither technology is new — convection ovens have been standard in professional kitchens for decades — but the air fryer repackaged it into a countertop appliance that is faster for everyday meals.

Air Fryer vs Convection Oven: Crispiness, Capacity, Speed and Cost

On crispiness, the basket air fryer wins for small batches. The TurboBlaze's tight chamber and rapid circulation brown fries and wings in 10–20 minutes with almost no preheat, and the perforated basket lets hot air reach every side. The Ninja DZ201 extends that advantage to family cooking: its Smart Finish feature syncs two zones so a main and a side finish at the same time, and Match Cook copies one zone's settings to the other for a full 8-quart batch — up to 4 lbs of fries or wings at once, according to the manufacturer. On capacity, the convection oven wins outright. The Breville Smart Oven Air Fryer Pro fits a 14-pound turkey or a 12-inch pizza in its 1-cubic-foot cavity, and the Instant Omni Plus fits a 12-inch pizza, six slices of bread or a whole chicken in its 18-litre interior — loads no basket air fryer can match. Baking is the clearest dividing line: cookies, cakes and bread need the even, gentle heat and the flat rack space of an oven, and the Breville's dedicated cookies and proofing functions are built for exactly that. If your cooking is mostly baking and roasting with occasional air frying, the oven-style models are the right buy.

On speed and cost, the basket air fryer wins the weeknight and the oven wins the weekend. A basket air fryer is cooking within a minute of being switched on; a convection oven needs several minutes to preheat, though far less than a wall oven. Running costs follow the same pattern: the Ninja draws 1,690 watts but finishes a batch quickly, while the Breville and Instant draw around 1,800 watts for longer sessions — still far less energy than heating a full-size oven for the same meal. On price, the TurboBlaze at $90–$140 is the cheapest way into air frying, the DZ201 at $170–$230 is the family upgrade, the Omni Plus at $180–$250 is the value oven, and the Breville at $350–$400 is the premium one. Which One Should You Buy? (And When Both Make Sense)

Buy a basket air fryer if you cook for one to three people, if your most-cooked foods are fries, wings, nuggets, vegetables and reheated leftovers, and if speed matters more than batch size. The COSORI TurboBlaze 6.0-Qt is the default pick: big enough for a family side dish, small enough to live on the counter, and cheap enough to be an easy Black Friday buy. Buy the Ninja Foodi DZ201 if you cook for four or more and are tired of cooking in rounds — two zones that finish together genuinely change weeknight dinners.

Buy a convection oven if you bake, roast whole chickens, cook for a crowd, or want one appliance that replaces both a toaster oven and an air fryer. The Instant Omni Plus is the value choice — 10 functions, rotisserie included, at roughly half the Breville's price. The Breville Smart Oven Air Fryer Pro is the premium choice: better temperature control, a larger and more even cavity, and the build quality to last years of daily use. Both are countertop ovens first and air fryers second, so expect slightly less aggressive crisping than a basket — the trade-off for all that capacity.

And yes, many kitchens end up with both, and that is the honest answer for keen cooks: a basket air fryer for fast, crispy weeknight sides and an oven-style model for roasts, baking and big family meals. If counter space allows only one, count your meals for a week: if most are small, fast and crispy, buy the basket; if most are large, baked or roasted, buy the oven. Either way, late November is the best time to buy — air fryers and countertop ovens are among the most heavily discounted small appliances of Black Friday Week. Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Match capacity to your household: 6 quarts suits 1–3 people; dual-basket or oven-style suits families of 4+.
  2. Check the temperature range: the Ninja's 105–450°F range covers dehydrating to high-heat crisping.
  3. Look for dishwasher-safe parts: baskets and crisper plates should go in the dishwasher — you will clean them daily.
  4. Measure your counter and cabinets: oven-style models are wide and tall, and need clearance above for heat.
  5. Do not overload the basket: food needs space for air to circulate — cook in batches or it steams.
  6. Buy in Black Friday Week: air fryers and countertop ovens see their deepest discounts of the year in late November.
