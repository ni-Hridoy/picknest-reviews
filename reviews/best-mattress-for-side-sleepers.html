Why does side sleeping need a special mattress at all? When you lie on your side, your weight concentrates on two relatively small contact points: the shoulder and the hip. On a mattress that is too firm, those points bear too much pressure, blood flow drops, and you wake up with numb arms or aching hips. On a mattress that is too soft, the waist sinks, the spine bends, and you wake up with lower-back pain. The ideal side-sleeper mattress threads the needle: soft enough at the shoulders and hips, firm enough at the waist. That is why the picks below all use either zoned support, thick pressure-relieving comfort layers, or both.

Buying Guide: 5 Things to Check Before You Buy

  1. Firmness for your weight: Under 130 lbs, aim for medium-soft (5/10); 130–230 lbs, medium (5.5–6.5/10); over 230 lbs, medium-firm (6.5–7/10) so hips do not sink too far.
  2. Zoned support: Look for firmer coils or foam at the waist and softer zones at the shoulders — this is the single most useful feature for side sleepers.
  3. Comfort layer thickness: At least 3 inches of pressure-relieving foam or latex above the support core, so shoulders and hips have room to sink.
  4. Cooling: If you sleep hot, choose a hybrid with coils and a breathable cover over dense all-foam; phase-change covers and ventilated foam help too.
  5. Trial length: Never buy a side-sleeper mattress with less than a 100-night trial. Give it 30 nights minimum — shoulder and hip pain from an old mattress takes weeks to fade.

Frequently Asked Questions

Most side sleepers do best on a medium-soft to medium mattress, around 5–7 on a 10-point firmness scale. That range lets the shoulders and hips sink enough to keep the spine straight. Heavier side sleepers usually need the firmer end of that range.

When you sleep on your side, your body weight concentrates on two small areas — the shoulder and the hip. A mattress that is too firm creates pressure points there, cutting off circulation and causing numbness or aching. A mattress with good pressure relief lets those areas sink in while supporting the waist.

Both can work. Memory foam excels at contouring and pressure relief, while hybrids add airflow, bounce and edge support. Side sleepers who run hot often prefer hybrids; side sleepers with sharp pressure points often prefer thick memory foam comfort layers.

Give it at least 30 nights. Your body needs time to adjust to a new support profile, and pressure-point pain from an old mattress can take weeks to fade. Use the full sleep trial — most brands offer 100–365 nights — before deciding.
