This is a research-based comparison of specs and published test results, not a hands-on test. The first decision in this category is not the brand — it is the construction. A weighted blanket is a blanket filled with evenly distributed heavy material that applies gentle, consistent pressure across the body, a sensation often described as deep touch pressure. There are two ways to build that weight: glass beads sewn into stitched compartments, or — in Bearaby's case — layers of hand-knit cotton yarn with no fill at all. Glass beads are dense, quiet and cheap, which is why the YnM and Baloo cost a fraction of the Bearaby. The second decision is weight, and the rule everyone cites is about 10 percent of your body weight. A 150 lb adult should look at a 15 lb blanket; a 120 lb adult at 12 lb; a 200 lb adult at 20 lb. The Sleep Foundation puts the comfortable range for a healthy adult at roughly 5 to 12 percent of body weight, and the practical advice from buying guides is consistent: treat 10 percent as the centre of a small range, not a prescription, and when two weights both look plausible, buy the lighter one — too much weight causes restlessness, which defeats the purpose, while too little weight just feels like an ordinary blanket. People who feel confined under weight, who toss and turn, or who sleep on their stomach often prefer a pound or two under the rule. What Is a Weighted Blanket, and How Does It Work?

A weighted blanket is a blanket with evenly distributed weight — usually glass beads, plastic pellets or, in Bearaby's case, dense knit cotton — sewn into compartments so the weight stays spread across the body. The idea is deep touch pressure: the even, gentle pressure feels like a firm hug, and that sensation is associated with the parasympathetic nervous system, the part of the body that handles rest and calm. Manufacturers and many owners describe falling asleep faster and waking less often under a weighted blanket. The honest position is that the research is thinner than the marketing suggests. One controlled trial found weighted blankets helped a narrow group of people with insomnia, but the wider evidence base stays thin, and the Sleep Foundation calls the research behind the claimed benefits fairly inconclusive. Treat a weighted blanket as a comfort aid that many people genuinely love, not as a medical treatment — and if you have a sleep disorder, talk to a clinician rather than buying a blanket and hoping.

Sizing is the detail most first-time buyers get wrong. A weighted blanket should cover one person, not one mattress. Weight that hangs over the edge of the bed pulls off the sleeper and onto the floor, so the rated pounds stop doing their work. That is why the Bearaby and the Baloo throw are sized to a single sleeper (40 x 72 in and 42 x 72 in) rather than to a bed, and why the queen-size YnM at 60 x 80 in suits a single sleeper who wants full coverage or a taller sleeper whose feet would otherwise escape a throw. Measure the sleeper, shoulders to feet, before you measure the bed. Care matters too: bead blankets need their covers washed regularly and the bead compartments checked for leaks, while the Bearaby goes in the machine whole but takes a long time to dry — Bearaby warns against top-loading machines and notes it may take more than one drying cycle. Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Start with the 10% rule: multiply your body weight by 0.1 and round down if you fall between sizes — too heavy causes restlessness, too light just feels ordinary.
  2. Pick the construction first: knit cotton (Bearaby) for breathability and no beads; glass beads (YnM, Baloo) for the lowest price and the widest weight range.
  3. Hot sleeper? Choose the Bearaby's open knit or the YnM's bamboo cover; avoid heavy bead blankets with polyester covers.
  4. Size it to the sleeper, not the bed: a throw or twin usually fits one adult; a queen suits a taller sleeper or someone who wants full coverage.
  5. Check the wash instructions: confirm whether the whole blanket is machine washable or only the cover — heavy blankets are hard to wash at home.
  6. Know the return window: Baloo allows 30 days, Bearaby offers a 1-year warranty, and YnM offers a 1-year warranty — buy from a seller with a clear return policy.
  7. Check the safety list: skip weighted blankets if you have breathing disorders, heart or circulation problems, sleep apnea or claustrophobia, and never use one on a small child.
