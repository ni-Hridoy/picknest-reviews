If your budget is tight, the Novilla 14-inch memory foam mattress is proof that you do not need to spend $800 to sleep well. Its four-layer construction — gel memory foam on top, comfort foam, a transition layer and a high-density support base — delivers genuine pressure relief at a price that often sits in the $270–$340 range for a queen. If you sleep hot or weigh over 230 pounds, saving up for a hybrid is probably the better long-term call — but for most average-weight sleepers on a budget, the Novilla is a genuinely good mattress, not just a cheap one. Pair it with a breathable protector and a cool room, and it punches well above its price.

Buying Guide: 5 Things to Check Before You Buy

  1. Firmness vs. your sleep position: Side sleepers need medium-soft to medium (5–7/10) for shoulder and hip cushioning; back sleepers do best at medium-firm (6–7/10); stomach sleepers need firm (7–8/10) to keep hips from sinking. See our side sleeper guide if you mainly sleep on your side.
  2. Hybrid or all-foam: Hybrids sleep cooler, bounce more and support more weight; all-foam isolates motion better and costs less. Hot sleepers and heavier sleepers should lean hybrid.
  3. Sleep trial and return terms: 100 nights is the minimum worth accepting; 365 nights is ideal. Confirm the trial start date, any break-in period, and whether return pickup is free.
  4. Warranty and materials: Look for at least a 10-year warranty and CertiPUR-US certified foams. Check foam density and coil counts rather than marketing claims.
  5. Timing and sizing: Buy during Black Friday or holiday sales for the deepest discounts, measure your bed frame and doorway, and give the mattress 24–72 hours to fully expand before judging it.
