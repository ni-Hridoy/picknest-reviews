Pet air is a three-part problem: dander (the tiny skin flakes that trigger allergies), hair (which clogs filters fast), and odour. A purifier built for pets needs to handle all three — a True HEPA filter for dander, a pre-filter for hair, and activated carbon for smell.

Coverage is the number most buyers get wrong. CADR — clean air delivery rate — and the square footage a purifier is rated for are measured at full speed, so a purifier rated for your exact room size will run loud to keep up. Buy one rated for a larger room than you have, and it will clean the air on a quieter, lower setting. The US EPA's guidance on air cleaners is a useful check: it recommends choosing a purifier with a CADR that matches your room and running it continuously for the best results. What Is Pet Dander, and How Do Air Purifiers Help?

Pet dander is not pet hair — it is the microscopic flakes of skin that cats and dogs shed constantly, and it is the main trigger for pet allergies. Dander particles are tiny, often under 10 microns, so they stay airborne for hours and settle into carpets, bedding and upholstery. That is why vacuuming alone does not solve pet allergies: the vacuum stirs dander up, and a purifier is what pulls it out of the air.

A True HEPA filter captures 99.97% of particles down to 0.3 microns, which comfortably includes dander, pollen and dust mite waste. The pre-filter's job is different: it catches the visible stuff — hair and large dust — so the HEPA filter does not clog in weeks. Activated carbon handles the third problem, odour, by adsorbing the volatile compounds that make a pet home smell like a pet home. The EPA notes that air cleaners work best as part of a broader approach: source control (grooming, washing bedding), ventilation, and a purifier sized for the room, running continuously. A robot vacuum that runs daily helps too — our best robot vacuum for pet hair guide covers the machines that keep pet hair off the floor in the first place.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Size up, not down: buy a purifier rated for a bigger room than yours, so it runs quietly on low.
  2. Check the filter cost: HEPA filters last 6–12 months; budget $30–$70 a year.
  3. Look for a washable pre-filter: it catches pet hair and doubles the HEPA filter's life.
  4. Run it continuously: dander is shed constantly — a purifier that runs only at night misses most of it.
  5. Place it right: put it where your pet sleeps, with space around it for airflow.
  6. Do not skip the vacuum: a purifier cleans air, not carpets — pair it with a good vacuum.
