What Makes a Good Pillow for Side Sleepers?

When you sleep on your side, your head sits a shoulder-width above the mattress. A pillow that is too low lets your head drop, bending your neck down; a pillow that is too high pushes your head up, bending your neck the other way. Either way you wake up stiff. The right pillow fills the shoulder gap exactly and holds your head level with your spine — and because shoulder width varies from person to person, the right loft is personal, which is why adjustable pillows dominate this category.

Fill matters too. Solid memory foam holds its shape but can trap heat and feels unforgiving if the loft is wrong. Shredded foam and latex noodles can be reshaped every night and let air move through the pillow, which keeps you cooler. Latex is the most responsive and durable of the fills here, and it resists dust mites naturally — a real consideration if you have allergies. And do not forget the second pillow: many side sleepers hug a pillow or put one between their knees, which keeps the hips aligned and takes pressure off the lower back. Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Measure your shoulder: broad shoulders need a higher loft; narrow shoulders need less.
  2. Choose adjustable: a pillow you can tune beats a fixed loft you have to guess.
  3. Pick the fill for your temperature: latex and shredded fills sleep cooler than solid memory foam.
  4. Check the trial: a pillow needs a week of nights to judge — look for at least 30 nights.
  5. Wash the cover: side sleeper pillows collect face oils; a washable cover extends the pillow's life.
  6. Replace every 1–2 years: even the best pillow loses support; if it does not spring back, it is done.
