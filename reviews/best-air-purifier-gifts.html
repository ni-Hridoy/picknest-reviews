Coway Mighty vs Levoit 600S vs Blueair: Sizing the Gift

The Coway Airmega Mighty is the default recommendation of independent testers for the better part of a decade, and the ideal gift size: CADR of 233 smoke / 246 dust / 240 pollen covers a bedroom up to about 360 square feet, its auto mode reacts to real-time air quality, and annual filters cost roughly $50. If you know nothing except that they have allergies and a bedroom, this is the one to wrap.

The Levoit Core 600S is the step-up for large rooms and open-plan living spaces, with coverage rated to 635 square feet and smart app and voice control — the right gift for the sufferer whose symptoms are worst in the living room, or for a big primary bedroom. The Blueair Blue Pure 211i Max is the premium, quiet option: its HEPASilent system moves a lot of air at remarkably low noise, which makes it the best choice for light sleepers and for homes where the purifier will run in shared spaces all day.

The Winix 5500-2 is the pet owner's allergy gift. If the allergy sufferer also has a dog or cat — a very common combination — the Winix is arguably the most thoughtful pick of the four.

Gifting Cleaner Air: Filters, Placement and Timing

Make the gift complete: add a replacement filter to the box. A purifier's HEPA filter lasts 6–12 months, and a spare removes the first year's running cost entirely — a small addition that makes a premium gift feel considered. Placement guidance is worth a line in the card, too: the bedroom, a few feet from the wall, running continuously on auto, is how a purifier earns its keep; allergy guidance generally targets 4–5 air changes per hour in the sleeping room.

Timing is on your side in late November. Air purifiers are heavily promoted through Black Friday Week — the Coway Mighty in particular regularly hits its lowest price of the year — and winter is peak indoor-allergy season, with windows closed and heating stirring dust. A purifier given at Christmas starts helping the same night.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. True HEPA only: avoid 'HEPA-type' filters — they are not held to the 99.97% standard.
  2. Match CADR to the room: dust/pollen CADR should be at least two-thirds of the room's square footage.
  3. Bedroom first: one purifier cleans one room — gift it for where they sleep.
  4. Check noise: sleep modes around 25 dB will not disturb light sleepers.
  5. Add a spare filter: it removes the first year's $50–$100 running cost from the gift.
  6. Buy in Black Friday Week: purifiers see their deepest discounts of the year in late November.
