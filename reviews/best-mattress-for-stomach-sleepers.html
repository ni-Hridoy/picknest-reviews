Stomach sleeping is the hardest position to shop for, because the thing that makes a mattress comfortable for a side sleeper — deep sinkage at the shoulders and hips — is exactly what hurts a stomach sleeper. When you lie face down, your hips are the heaviest part of your body, and if they sink below your shoulders your lower back arches all night. That is why the fix is not complicated but it is specific: a firmer surface, usually 7 to 8 on the standard 10-point firmness scale, keeps the pelvis level with the rest of the torso so the spine stays in a neutral line.

What Makes a Mattress Good for Stomach Sleepers?

Stomach sleeping is the least common sleep position, and mattress design mostly ignores it — which is why so many stomach sleepers wake up with lower-back pain and assume the problem is their back. More often the problem is the bed. When you sleep on your stomach, gravity pulls your midsection down into the mattress. On a soft bed the hips sink several inches below the shoulders, the lumbar spine hyperextends, and the muscles along the lower back spend all night working to hold things together. You wake up stiff, and the stiffness gets blamed on age, or the pillow, or the way you slept. A firm mattress prevents the sink in the first place: the surface pushes back against the hips, the pelvis stays level with the torso, and the spine holds a neutral line.

Firmness is the first thing to get right, and the number to look for is 7 to 8 out of 10. Below 7, most stomach sleepers sink too far; above 8, the bed can feel like a board and put pressure on the ribs and knees. Body weight shifts the target: lighter stomach sleepers (under 130 lb) can often manage with a 7 because they do not sink as far, while heavier stomach sleepers (over 230 lb) usually need a true 8. That is why the Saatva and Nolah picks matter — both let you choose the firmness at checkout instead of hoping a single fixed feel happens to be firm enough. Support construction is the second thing: pocketed coils and zoned support hold the midsection up better than a uniform foam core, which is why all three of our picks are hybrids. Thickness matters too — a taller mattress with more comfort layers can still be firm, but a thin, soft foam topper on a firm base is a recipe for hip sink. Finally, the pillow matters as much as the mattress: stomach sleepers need a thin, low-loft pillow, or no pillow at all, so the neck is not forced upward. A thick pillow plus a firm mattress is a common combination that still causes neck pain.

Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Match firmness to your weight: under 130 lb, a 7 out of 10 is usually enough; 130–230 lb, aim for 7–8; over 230 lb, buy the firmest option available.
  2. Choose a hybrid over all-foam: coils push back against the hips; a soft foam core lets them sink.
  3. Check the trial terms: all three picks offer at least 100 nights — use at least three weeks before deciding, since firm beds take time to adjust to.
  4. Ask about the return fee: Saatva charges $99 for a return; Helix and Nolah terms vary by promotion, so confirm before ordering.
  5. Measure your bed frame and sheets: the Nolah is 15 inches tall and needs deep-pocket sheets; the Saatva 14.5-inch height does not work on some adjustable bases.
  6. Buy a thin pillow with it: a low-loft pillow keeps your neck neutral on a firm stomach-sleeper mattress.
  7. Shop Black Friday week: late November is the strongest mattress sale window of the year, and all three brands run deep discounts.
