What Is Cold Brew, and How Does It Work?

Cold brew is coffee steeped in cold or room-temperature water for 12 to 24 hours instead of being brewed with heat. Time replaces temperature: the long, slow extraction pulls out the coffee's sweet, chocolatey compounds while leaving behind many of the bitter, acidic compounds that hot water extracts quickly. The result is a smooth, naturally sweet concentrate that you dilute with water or milk — usually one part concentrate to one or two parts water — and serve over ice or heated.

The grind is the single biggest variable. Cold brew needs a coarse grind, about the texture of raw sugar: too fine, and the grounds over-extract and clog the filter, leaving a muddy, bitter brew. The ratio matters too — most makers use roughly one part coffee to four or five parts water by weight. And because the brew is a concentrate, it keeps well: sealed in the fridge, it stays fresh for up to two weeks, which is why a Sunday batch can cover the whole work week. Buying Guide: What to Check Before You Buy

  1. Match the batch to your household: one person needs the OXO Compact; a family needs the Takeya or Toddy.
  2. Grind coarse: cold brew needs a coarse, even grind — a burr grinder beats a blade grinder here.
  3. Use filtered water: cold brew is mostly water, and chlorine or hard-water flavours show through.
  4. Steep 12–24 hours: under 12 hours tastes weak and grassy; over 24 hours it turns bitter.
  5. Check the filter type: mesh is free forever; felt (Toddy) needs washing and replacing.
  6. Measure your fridge space: the OXO Compact is designed to fit a fridge door; the Toddy needs a shelf.
